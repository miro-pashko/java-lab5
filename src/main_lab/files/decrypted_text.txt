The Hollywood star played a drug dealer in this dark, claustrophobic thriller.
For the BBC's Close Up Film series, he reflects on the surprising part that means the most to him in his career.
The painfully shy schoolboy who finds his voice in Dead Poets Society.
The cynical musician in Reality Bites, which made him a poster boy for Generation X.
The doubt-filled alcoholic priest of First Reformed.
And the brilliant but wounded lyricist Lorenz Hart in Blue Moon, which gave him his first best actor Oscar nomination at the age of 54.
They are among the many roles that Ethan Hawke has played during a 40-year acting career, and this week sees the UK release of his latest film, The Weight, an adventure drama set during the Great Depression.
Hawke plays a convict, Murphy, who strikes a deal to smuggle gold through the Oregon wilderness in exchange for being freed from a labour camp.
But when asked to name the film role that has been most significant to him, he chooses a surprising film from his extensive CV.
"If I had to pick a film role that changed my life, it would be Tape," Hawke tells the BBC, referring to the low-budget claustrophobic 2001 drama he made with his regular collaborator, director Richard Linklater.
